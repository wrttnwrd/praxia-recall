## Description

A torn, tattered fragment of what appears to be the scholarly lecture notes of an unknown author.

Date translates to 200 years before [the Fall](/world/events/the-fall.md).

## Who Was Thargorax?

Theories abound, of course, on the origin, fate, and goals of the Sequar Deones (also known as The Later Gods or, if you are a stuffy sort - Hieronymus, you know you are - Those That Followed). 

[The Tome Of The First Gods](/world/things/tome-of-the-first-gods.md) is the seminal work, and for our purposes I shall use that as the foundation.

Even with the Tome however we do not know much of Thargorax. A relatively minor Later God, she nevertheless spent a great deal of time walking [Praxia](/world/places/praxia.md) in various forms. 

In the months and weeks leading up to the First War we have in the historical record at least fifty sightings. If accurate, that makes her by far the most active of the Sequar Deones.

She visited every continent and every known settlement. She led the recruitment of the Later Gods' army.

Why was she the leader? Because of her ability to take many forms? Unlikely. At the time, most of the Deones could do that. 

I suspect Thargorax could best "press the flesh" as Bartholemina often puts it. Of all of the gods, she was most like the mortals and elves. She could best speak to them, promise them what they wanted, and persuade them to join the fight against the Biran Deones (the Second Gods, students, keep up, this will be on the exam).

Think of this: You are back behind the quad, sharing a wineskin (come now, I was young once - you **do** still drink wine in the Whispering Goblet Society, don't you?) and I walk up to you. What happens? The wineskin is swiftly hidden. Giggles stifled. Everyone is cordial but you know you cannot quite trust me. I am too different - too, well, old.

Now, a different situation: Again, you are sharing one of the acidic, silty vintages available for underage students (oh, really, don't look so awkward, everyone knows, no one gets in trouble - better wine than Fool's Bloom), and Vilma Norova approaches. She is a professor, true. But she's younger and - I am allowed to say this as a 300-year-old Dwarf who resembles a hairy toadstool left too long in the rain - quite handsome. She speaks plainly, using your vernacular. She may even offer better wine. Are you entirely at ease? Perhaps not. But you are receptive. You open the circle and let her sit. You may chat with her.

This was Thargorax. No no no people I am not suggesting Professor Norova is a deity bent upon the destruction of the Second Gods. Which are also called what? YES! The Biran Deones. Mr. Kelrin, you get an A.

Thargorax was accessible. She could think like the mortals and elves. She knew what they wanted. Each of her aspects was suited to the strengths and weaknesses of the peoples of [Praxia](/world/places/praxia.md).

And, she learned something of the worldly pleasures of those peoples. Food. Drink. Whatever else it is that you younger folk do.

## What Happened To Her
If you understand *why* Thargorax walked [Praxia](/world/places/praxia.md) so often, what happened to her becomes easier to understand.

She was the *only* Later God who fought in front of the Stygian Gate. She unfurled the Door That Opens and brought the Gate into being. She was struck and flung through the gate because *she was there.*

So, unlike the others, she could not flee. She was trapped on the other side of the Gate on whatever plane she landed. With her went her strongest lieutenants and servants.

You can only imagine her rage and fear. I think the expression you all would use is, "Royally pissed off."

## Why We Care
Pretend for a moment this is not all myth. We have seen the Stygian Gate reappear from time to time. Extraplanar beings do return to [Praxia](/world/places/praxia.md), sometimes for good, sometimes for ill. 

The story is that the Later Gods used the Door That Opens to tear the Stygian Gate through to the Abyss. They wished to unleash a demonic horde upon the Second City. They failed, and there Thargorax fell. There she remains.

Thargorax is still alive. Only the Second Gods can truly destroy the Later Gods, and they seem to avoid that.

She cannot possibly be happy so far separated from the followers and finer things that brought her so much satisfaction. 

But how could she return? The veil between planes thicken. [The Seals](/world/things/the-seals.md), as you know, are less important. 

She would need help. To pierce the veil, she must first weaken it. To open the Stygian Gate, she will need powerful talismans: The Door That Opens. The Hand That Strikes. And so on.

She will need her lieutenants. Long dead now, of course, but we've all read Ekelethon's treatise On The Nature Of Aspects, so that's a laughably small hurdle.

And if she returned, what would she do? Burn the planet? Enslave us all? Feast upon all the delights she's missed, mayhap. 

But that is so very boring, yes? 

If Thargorax **is** in many ways like us, then she loves and fears and hates. She bears a grudge. For 3,000 years she has lived and suffered in [Avernus](/world/places/avernus.md). She has nursed resentment of the Second Gods and still seeks to throw them down. 

If Thargorax returns, it will be to make war on the Biran Deones. Where that will leave us all, I cannot say.

I think this will happen, soon. If not in my lifetime, then in yours. If not in yours, then in your children's. Historically speaking, a blink of an eye.

Do not simply accept what I am saying. Read the texts, study, think, and decide for yourselves. 

*Recorder's note: We now know that Thargorax is one aspect of the demon goddess [Tiamat](/world/people/tiamat.md)*
