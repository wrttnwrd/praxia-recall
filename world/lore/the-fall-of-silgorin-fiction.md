The city of [Silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md) was doomed.

A cold iron chain, each link the size of an elephant, dangled from the city as it floated impossibly in the cindered sky of [Avernus](/world/places/avernus.md). The chain vanished into the reeking dark of the River Styx where hellish machinery dragged the chain, and with it Silgorin, into ice-cold waters. Inevitably, Silgorin would vanish, its surviving citizens swallowed up.

A pier extended from a black basalt quay out to the chain, and all around it fiends, mortals, and monsters battled for control over the souls of Silgorin. The ground and decks of ships were littered with pools of ichor, piles of ash, discarded weapons. The din of battle and the reek of a dozen varieties of blood pummeled onlookers. Over it all, a thousand voices cried out in despair. It left you witless.

As he stood on the quay, Rikterr Shepherd's mailed fist helplessly clenched and unclenched Bastion's hilt. This wasn't **his** fight. He felt the all-to-familiar struggle between his head and his heart: Win the war, or fight the battle before his eyes? 

His head was losing. 

*Rage has etched lines in his flesh,* Mossy thought, eyeing her friend. Her attention was divided. On the quay, six chain-covered devils guarded the pier that was the one secure path to the chain, watching the whirling battles around them. The devils were gray and grim and carried barbed flails the size of a man's head.

"Come on," she hissed through clenched teeth, "we need to move before they notice us."

Mossy, Rikterr and their three companions were too conspicuous. Even with the Badge Of Zariel, they were attracting unwanted attention. They needed to get to their boat and set sail or risk a long, hard fight.

Rikterr didn't budge.

"We could stop this, right now," he snapped, "We could kill everyone here, break the chain, and then be on our way."

"We could, my friend, but we'd lose all chance of surprising Tiamat. And they've got an endless supply of baddies. Remember why we're here," she grabbed his elbow, "Let's move."

Too late. Three of the chain devils detached themselves from the group and walked towards them, saying something in Abyssal. 

Sango translated, silently relaying to the group.

"They want to know why we're waiting here. They're not suspicious, though. We should just move on."

Rikterr jerked his arm from Mossy's grasp. Madness grew in his eyes. 

"Bullshit. You'd say anything to get me moving. This is what they do," he whirled on the party, "pushing us to the next battle, putting evil right in front of us, forcing us to choose. I'm not choosing anymore."

The leader of the devils gestured towards Rikterr and said something. Again, Sango translated.

"He wants us to leave. Now."

The leader stepped closer, brandishing his flail. He made a mistake, poking at Rikterr while saying something that needed no translation. 

It was one step too far. Rikterr roared, hewing down the offender and hacking the arm off another. He spun, blocking the third as it tried to brain him with an arcing flail strike.

Mossy cursed, then flipped a dagger into the one-armed devil. Drak cut down the third with a well-placed arrow. 

Cregyn looked around. They hadn't attracted much attention. So far, the chaos around them was working in their favor. This was one more skirmish in the midst of a pitched battle. But that was bound to change.

"Gods dammit," Drak said silently over the Party Line, "Rikterr, the city's got days. We can come back."

And just like that, their luck ran out. Five more chain devils saw Rikterr, surrounded by bodies. They drew their weapons and charged.

Sango's pirouetting kick floored one of the new attackers. Then a curtain of darkness slammed down over the group. RBG, now hidden, got to work.

Drak's long knives slashed the leg tendons of one devil, leaving it howling on the ground for Cregyn, who crushed its skull while sending a ray of force into another. Mossy rolled a clockwork device toward a charging devil. The device flared, wreathing the victim in green-orange flames. Sango punched it in the throat and left it on the ground, where it turned to ash.

One of the remaining chain devils spun, leveling its flail at Mossy. Rikterr blocked the blow while Mossy rolled under his shield and gutted her attacker. Rikterr didn't even notice as a devil's flail struck home, leaving a huge dent in the armor on his side. With a gesture, Cregyn bade Rikterr's broken ribs to knit. Drak used a whip of thorns to drag the last attacker back into the center of the darkness, where Sango and Mossy quickly dispatched it.

It was over in moments.

But a noisy battle inside an isolated, lightless globe attracted a whole other kind of attention. 

Nine feet tall, bristling with long, white spikes, bone devils dove into the fray, heedlessly plunging spears into the darkness. With the guards distracted, a riot of demons and devils poured from the decks of ships, jamming the pier. 

Sango dropped the darkness as he spun to face the new attackers. Cregyn and Mossy started cutting down bone devils. 

But Rikterr saw the approaching mob on the pier. He ground his teeth, eyes narrowing. He drew out his belt pouch, leaving it on the pier, unnoticed. He stepped towards the fiends, Bastion ready in one hand, shield in the other.

"What are you doing?!!!" Sango shouted. 

Rikterr spared one glance back at his friends, then attacked.

The narrow pier meant he only had to face two to three opponents at a time, and the fiends on the pier were as busy fighting each other as him. Even the most disciplined devils found themselves in a chaotic brawl. This wasn't their kind of fight, and Rikterr went through them as if they were clay.

On the quay, Mossy, Sango, Cregyn, and Drak had dealt with the bone devils but had new a problem. A larger, organized group of devils had them surrounded. The Badge Of Zariel held them back, confused, but it wouldn't last. They were in for it.

"We are here for our ship, that's all," Sango said to the new leader, a long, spined devil carrying a zweihander, "We'll move on."

"No fighting on the quay," the spined devil rasped, "You need to come with us."

On the pier, Rikterr grinned madly, Bastion blurring as it hewed through combatants. The chain grew closer. He was halfway there. Rikterr called on the Raven Queen, vanishing from sight to reappear and hack down a shapeless demon that babbled in a dozen wet voices as it dissolved. Then he vanished again, aparating at the end of the pier. 

Confused, the devils and demons fell on each other, tearing and stabbing. 

The chain was so close he could touch it. It thrummed with tension.

Rikterr reached for the plain, platinum band he wore on his right hand and slipped it off. He held it against the chain, whispered a prayer, then slammed Bastion on it with all his might.

"No," Cregyn said, shielding his eyes.

A light flared, so bright you could see it through squeezed eyelids and closed helm. A searing wave of heat blasted across the battlefield. For the first time in millennia, the thick waters of the River Styx roiled, steaming, as the ships surrounding the chain turned to ash.

The survivors slowly opened their eyes, blinking away tears and dust. On the quay, the spine devils, previously holding them at spearpoint, lay senseless. At the end of the pier, the chain had split in two. Silgorin was now hovering, neither descending nor ascending, hundreds of feet above the river. 

Mossy's hands flew to her mouth. Sango, Drak, and Cregyn stared dumbfounded. Rikterr's burnt body followed the wrecked chain as it slid into the River Styx. The waters closed over his head, and just for a moment, just for them, silence fell over the battlefield. 

Silgorin was freed. Their friend was gone.

Mortified with grief, the four adventurers picked their way over stunned and slain fiends, on to their fated meeting with the queen of chromatic dragons.
