[Hull Ward](/world/places/hull-ward.md) greeted the new day with its usual riot of roosters and livestock turned into the streets to munch on leftovers from the previous night’s revels. Rain hissed down on [Aventine](/world/places/aventine.md)’s wealthier set as they tried to make a discrete exit from the Ward’s inns and whorehouses. 

Another sound intruded, though, like a hand slapping on a thigh in time to music. Footfalls on wet tile.

[Gritch](/world/people/gritch.md) sprinted, boots gripping the slick tile as she lept from one rooftop to the next. Rain ran off her shaved head as she flicked her eyes to the left and right, checking for an unlikely ambush. Unlikely, until that one time someone slashes your leg as you plunge blindly ahead. She’d learned that lesson more than once.

A panicky arrow clattered across the roof and landed at her feet. She ignored it. Just ahead, a tall figure in outrageously flamboyant lace dropped a short bow and clambered over the peak of the steep, slippery roof on which he stood. He dropped from view.

[Gritch](/world/people/gritch.md) glanced at the bow. It was as absurd as the owner, ornately carved and so curved it could barely propel an arrow, never mind injure someone. She didn’t slow as she hurdled the gap to the same roof, then lept up to the peak in two strides. As she reached the peak she dropped onto her right side, sliding sideways on one leg over and down the other side of the roof. A rapier stabbed at the empty air where her chest should have been. 

She saw her target, framed in the gray dawn. His lunge threw him off balance. Gritch used her right hand to cushion her fall, then killed her momentum by grabbing at one velvet boot with her left. The man’s legs flew out from under him and he smacked into the tile roof face-first. He let out dismayed grunt, then slid down the pitched tile on his belly and vanished from sight, still clutching his rapier.

It was only six feet from the edge of the sagging eave to the ground, but six feet is a long way for the unprepared. The fop landed on the soaked cobbles with a slap.

Gritch dropped silently next to him. He was simultaneously groaning and trying to gasp out a cry for help. 

He rolled onto his back and went silent. She studied him with cold, green-yellow eyes. His face was a greyish-red mask of blood, street refuse and fear. His weapon hand was askew, shattered by the fall. His other hand flailed in front of him as if to fend off her attack, then fell limp. He panted, eyes wide and rolling.

It always surprised her, the way commissions died. The stories said victims pleaded for their lives, offering money, slaves, and stature, or screaming curses at their killers. In her experience, they behaved more like livestock, staring stupidly right up to the moment the light left their eyes. She’d once had a woman swallow a mouthful of bread even as a blade penetrated her neck.

This man was no different. As she forced his head to the side with her boot and pressed, his hands reached up to her reflexively and twitched. His legs kicked twice, then went limp as his skull crunched wetly under her foot.

Messy, but the result looked as if he’d been trampled by a horse. A common enough fate for drunk, wealthy second sons whoring their way through Hull Ward. As requested.

She wiped off her boot on a nearby pile of straw and walked away without a backward glance, cloak over her head to protect from the cold rain.
