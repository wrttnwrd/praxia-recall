# The Silence of Amoth

A one-session murder mystery in the Temple of Amoth in Silgorin. Three clerics dead. The killer is the High Hierophant, who is going mad as his god dies — an event the players will witness tonight.

## DM truth (do not share)

Right now, somewhere very far above Praxia, **Asmodeus is killing Amoth**. The murder is not instantaneous. Amoth has been dying for weeks — slowly, in the way a vast god dies — and the only mortal in Silgorin close enough to him to feel it is **High Hierophant Veyrin Toldain**, the most senior cleric of the Temple of Amoth.

What Veyrin hears in his god's voice is *grief, then panic, then a thinning silence.* It has driven him out of his mind. He has come to believe his fellow clerics, who go on chanting the rote services, are **drowning out** Amoth's last words with their false, empty piety. He has been walking the temple at night, beating his brothers and sisters to death with the ceremonial maul of the order — **the Hand of Amoth** — to clear the air.

Tonight, while the players are inside the temple, **Amoth dies**. The cosmic shock of the murder tears a hole between this city and [avernus](/world/places/avernus.md). **The city is dragged through it.** Most of Silgorin — every street, every house, every soul — is gone in a single moment. Only the **Temple of Amoth** and a few buildings on the highest part of the bluff are spared, left perched on the basalt rim of a vast new crater that opens directly onto the Nine Hells.

The Helgoril, no longer flowing out of springs that no longer exist, will reverse over the next hours and begin pouring **east-to-west** down into the crater. That crater becomes the-gurges. The surviving rim — the temple plus the few buildings — becomes the founding of [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md).

**Your players are about to become founders of Silgorin.** They will not understand this tonight. The session ends with them on the rim, looking down.

## Pitch (read this to the players)

> Three clerics of Amoth are dead in a week. Each found alone, in different parts of the temple, beaten so badly the bones broke and the stones beneath them stained. No forced entry. No witnesses. **The Black Helms can't go in badge-up — the city would riot — but the High Hierophant has signed off on four "discreet officers" in plain clothes.** That's you. Lt. Aellis put your names on the letter. The High Rider's office has authorized a **50 gp commendation** each and formal recognition from the temple for closing the case before another body falls.

## Hook

The party are **Black Helms** — Silgorin's city watch — known internally for their discretion (which is why they keep getting the awkward jobs). Their lieutenant, **Brona Aellis** (lawful neutral, blunt, exhausted, half-elf, mid-30s), pulls them off their normal beats at shift change.

Three clerics dead. The Temple of Amoth will not allow the Helms to mount an **open** investigation — the city would not stand for the watch tramping through the sanctum in uniform — but the High Hierophant has agreed, in a letter signed in his own hand, to admit four discreet officers in their off-duty clothes. That's the party. They go in quietly. They report back **only to Aellis**.

Pay: standard duty plus a **50 gp commendation** each on completion (out of Aellis's discretionary budget). Time pressure: another body likely within 48 hours.

If the party drags their feet, Aellis reminds them this is the kind of case that gets careers made. She is not asking.

## Setting (lightweight)

Silgorin sits high on a stone bluff at the **headwaters of the Helgoril**, in the highlands of [essedria](/world/places/essedria.md) — far up the river from the lowlands and far west of the Sedruth Sea. The Helgoril emerges from springs in the rocks just below the city, gathers itself in a series of cold pools, and then begins its long eastward run to the ocean. The city is lit by **the Companion** — a captive sun hanging above the upper city. The **Hellriders** patrol the high ground; the **Black Helms** keep order in the streets (**the party serves in the Black Helms** — see Hook). **High Observer Thavius Kreeg** is the senior religious figure in the city (he runs the Hall of the Companion), but the **Temple of Amoth** is older and operates parallel to him. (DM aside: Kreeg is secretly a follower of Asmodeus. He will be **conspicuously calm** if the players cross his path tonight, even pleased. He knows what's coming.)

The Temple of Amoth occupies a tiered building of pale stone in **Highshelf**, near the cliff edge **overlooking the Helgoril valley**. Its great bronze doors face the rising sun. Above its inner sanctum hangs an **eternal flame** in a hammered-gold reliquary.

> **DM note on geography (DM-only)**: this city is built directly above the place where Amoth's worship is most concentrated on the planet. Tonight, when Amoth dies, the cosmic shock will tear a hole between this site and [avernus](/world/places/avernus.md). **The city is going to be dragged through.** What will be left on the rim of the resulting crater — the **Temple of Amoth** and a few surrounding buildings — is what your players will recognize, in another campaign someday, as the founding of [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md). The Helgoril, no longer flowing out of springs that no longer exist, will reverse and pour east-to-west into the new crater, which will become the-gurges. **Do not foreshadow this.** The players should believe, like everyone else in the city, that they are simply solving a murder.

### Veyrin's two faces (DM-only)

Veyrin in **daylight** is the man the party meets — gracious, gentle, witty, deeply concerned for his temple. This is not an act. It is what is left of him during the hours when his god is not actively dying in his mind.

Veyrin at **night** is something else. Once the temple is dark and the chants have stopped, the silence presses in on him and he goes — *not* into a fugue or a possession, but into a kind of awful clarity. He hears Amoth dying. He believes, *correctly*, that he alone can hear it. He believes, *wrongly*, that his fellow clerics' empty piety is drowning out the god's last words. So he hunts them, one at a time, and clears the air with the **Hand of Amoth**.

In the morning he remembers what he did but cannot quite reach it — like the memory of a nightmare. He buries the maul back in its case, washes his vestments, and is again the host the party will meet.

**The party should never see this fracture by daylight.** The fracture should be inferred entirely from the physical evidence and the witness statements (all of which are nocturnal).

### Victims (already dead)

- **Brother Alos** — initiate. Found in the cloister garden three nights ago.
- **Sister Elenia** — herbalist. Found near the kitchens five nights ago.
- **Brother Cailen** — junior cleric. Found at the foot of the bell tower stairs this morning.

All three killed with crushing blows from a heavy object. **Broken bones, no lacerations.** No defensive wounds — they were struck from behind, by someone they trusted enough to turn their back on.

## Locations inside the temple

### The nave

Pale stone, soaring vaults, the eternal flame burning above the altar. Worshippers come and go in the day; mostly empty at night. Veyrin spends most of his daylight hours here, going through the motions of services he can no longer feel.

### The cloister garden

Where Brother Alos was killed. A low fountain in the center. Bloodstains on the flagstones — partly washed away. **DC 12 Investigation**: the splatter pattern shows Alos was struck from behind, fell forward, and was struck again on the ground. **DC 15 Investigation**: small flecks of **gold leaf and inlaid silver** in the wound — matching the temple's ceremonial maul.

### The kitchens

Where Sister Elenia was killed at her herb-grinding bench. Black Helm chalk outline still on the floor. **DC 13 Perception**: a single muddy footprint on the stairs leading away — the **rosette pattern** of a hierophant's ritual sandal. Only Veyrin and Brother Halis wear them.

### The bell tower stairs

Where Brother Cailen was killed this morning. The body has been moved but the stones are still wet from washing. **Acolyte Mira** is found here, sitting against the wall, refusing to go upstairs.

### The reliquary

A small chamber off the nave. A glass case displays **the Hand of Amoth** — a heavy mithril-and-gold maul, forged in the founding of the temple, used for ceremonial blessings. **The case is open.** The maul is on its velvet cushion. **DC 13 Investigation**: dried blood is still in the inlay grooves of the head. The lock has been opened cleanly — by someone with the key.

Only **two people** have the key: the High Hierophant, and Brother Halis.

### Veyrin's private cell

If the players talk their way in (or break in): the cell is **immaculate**. Bed made. Books shelved by spine height. A single tea service on the desk, washed and dry. Nothing on the surface betrays him.

The writings are hidden. **DC 16 Investigation** finds a loose stone in the floor under the prayer kneeler. Inside: a folded sheaf of pages in his own hand. A handful of coherent excerpts the players can read:

> *"The voice is going under. I hear water in his throat."*
>
> *"They sang the Veneration today and felt nothing. I watched their mouths move and I knew. They are stopping his ears with their lies. They must be quiet so I can hear him die."*
>
> *"Cailen knew. I saw it in his face. He turned away from me and I struck him before he could speak the silence aloud."*

A successful **DC 14 Religion** check identifies the writing as Veyrin's hand (compare against signed missives from the temple).

### The inner sanctum (final encounter)

Above the altar, the eternal flame. Behind the altar, a stair down to a small private prayer chamber — Veyrin's place of last resort. This is where the players will find him at the end.

## Random encounter table

This is a temple, not a dungeon — and the climax is the unwinnable fight. **No combat on this table.** These are atmospheric moments, slivers of clues, and humanizing NPC beats. Their job is to fill transitions, raise the dread, and make the survivors on the rim feel like people the party knew.

Roll **1d12** once when the party first enters the temple. Roll again whenever they move between locations or the energy at the table flags. Skip any result that has already happened, that contradicts something established, or that doesn't fit the moment. Most entries are 30–90 seconds of play.

Three of the entries (marked **★**) are quiet cosmological tells — the dying god leaking through reality. Don't cluster them; if you roll a second ★ in the same scene, take the next result instead.

The point is to make the temple feel inhabited *now*, so the rim feels emptied *then*.

## The clue trail

The party doesn't need to find every clue. Two solid threads will reach Veyrin:

1. **The maul → the key → Veyrin.** Bloody Hand of Amoth → only two key-holders → eliminate Brother Halis (he was at a hospice all night when Cailen was killed; the hospice's matron, **Sister Imola**, will confirm) → only Veyrin is left.
2. **The witnesses converge.** Mira saw Veyrin standing over Cailen. Yura heard him chanting in tongues. Tenum saw the bloody vestments. Any one is suggestive; two together are damning.

Time pressure: if the party stalls past two hours, **Acolyte Mira disappears**. (Veyrin took her into the bell tower to "talk," intending to silence the last witness. The party finds her bound and gagged in the bell-loft when they go up — she's alive but terrified.)

## The confrontation

Players find Veyrin in **the inner sanctum** or the **prayer chamber below it**, kneeling at the altar, the Hand of Amoth across his knees, blood on his vestments. He does not look up when they enter.

Read or paraphrase:

> *"You hear it now, don't you. The hush. He is speaking and they are drowning him out. The chants. The bells. The empty mouths. I tried. I tried to give him quiet. To let him say the last thing. But there are too many of them and they will not stop and now the silence is too loud."*

He explains, plainly and exhaustedly, that Amoth is dying — has been dying for weeks. He says he killed his brothers and sisters because their false faith was deafening him to the god's last words. **He is not lying. He is, in fact, the only mortal in the city who is right about what is happening.**

### How to resolve

The party's options are real, but unequal:

- **Combat.** He attacks rather than be taken. **He will kill them.** Veyrin is far too powerful for four lvl-2 PCs and the fight is genuinely unwinnable by force. *They are not supposed to win it by force.* See #The fight.
- **Surrender.** A **DC 18 Persuasion** with advantage if a cleric, paladin, or anyone visibly holy is in the party. He drops the maul and weeps. He does not resist arrest.
- **Restrain.** Grapple/manacle. If they catch him before he attacks, he's exhausted enough to not resist hard. Skill challenge if the DM prefers, or just narrate.

**Whichever path, [The Fall](/world/events/the-fall.md) happens before the scene ends — and it ends the scene whether or not the players have been knocked out.**

## The fight

This fight is **deliberately unwinnable** for four lvl-2 PCs. Veyrin is the most powerful Amoth-cleric on the planet at the precise moment Amoth's death-spasm is feeding him every dying scrap of divine force. He opens with **Spirit Guardians** already up, **Spiritual Weapon** already manifested, and is flanked by **two Acolytes** who don't yet understand what's happening but will defend their hierophant on reflex.

The party will, on their own, lose. **The Fall is what saves them.** See the trigger below.

### High Hierophant Veyrin Toldain (modified Priest, overpowered for the moment)

- **AC** 18 (chain + shield)
- **HP** 60
- **Speed** 30 ft.
- **STR** 14 (+2) · **DEX** 10 · **CON** 14 (+2) · **INT** 12 · **WIS** 18 (+4) · **CHA** 14 (+2)
- **Skills** Religion +4, Insight +6
- **Spell save DC** 14 · **Spell attack** +6
- **Saving Throws** WIS +6, CHA +4
- **Damage Resistances** radiant (Amoth is dying *into* him)
- **Challenge** **roughly 6** while at full strength. Treat any combat as **Deadly+** for the party.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack.** Veyrin makes one attack with the Hand of Amoth and one attack with his Spiritual Weapon (as a bonus action).
- **The Hand of Amoth** (heavy maul). Melee, +6 to hit, reach 5 ft. **Hit:** 9 (1d10+2) bludgeoning, plus **18 (4d8) radiant** on the first hit each turn (Spirit Guardians).
- **Spiritual Weapon** (floating maul, bonus action). Melee spell, +6 to hit, reach within 60 ft. **Hit:** 7 (1d8+3) force.
- **Sacred Flame** (cantrip). Target makes a DC 14 DEX save or takes **2d8 radiant** (no cover bonus).
- **Hold Person** (1/scene). DC 14 WIS save vs. paralysis on a single humanoid in 60 ft.
- **Healing Word** (3/scene). Bonus action — heal himself or an Acolyte for 1d4+4 hp at 60 ft.
- **Cure Wounds** (2/scene). Touch — heal himself for 1d8+4 hp.

**Active at the start of combat**

- **Spirit Guardians** *(concentration; 4d8 radiant on creatures within 15 ft on their first turn or that start their turn there; DC 14 WIS save halves; speed halved on a failed save)*. He cast it the moment he heard the party at the sanctum door.
- **Spiritual Weapon** *(no concentration; bonus-action attack each turn)*.
- **Bless** *(concentration — note he can only concentrate on one of these at a time; if you want him at maximum brutality, drop Bless and keep Spirit Guardians)*. (Default: only Spirit Guardians active.)

**Tactics.** He moves to keep at least two PCs inside the Spirit Guardians aura. He uses Spiritual Weapon on whichever PC is doing the most damage. He uses Hold Person on the loudest holy/martial PC. He saves Healing Word for an Acolyte that takes a critical hit. He does not retreat.

### Two Acolytes

- **AC** 10 · **HP** 9 · standard MM Acolyte.
- **Sacred Flame** (DC 13, 1d8 radiant) every turn against any PC engaging Veyrin.
- They will **flee or surrender** the moment the Fall hits and Veyrin's powers fail. They are not the enemy. (See trigger below.)

### Amoth's Death (the trigger)

This is the heart of the encounter. **There is no encounter clock.** There is one trigger, and it fires when the party is genuinely about to die. Use whichever signal hits first:

- **The end of any round in which a second PC drops to 0 hp**, OR
- **The end of round 3**, if the dice have not already chewed through the party, OR
- **DM's call**, the moment one more attack from Veyrin would TPK the table.

When the trigger fires, **stop the fight on Veyrin's initiative — before he can act**. Read [The Fall](/world/events/the-fall.md) (below). Do not roll any more dice for Veyrin or the Acolytes. The Fall is the resolution.

If the players are unconscious / dying when the Fall hits, that is fine. **They are not going to die in this scenario unless the DM chooses to let them.** Ground-shock and the temple's collapse will not finish them. The Fall does not target them — it takes the city. The temple stays. They wake later, broken-bodied but alive, on the rim.

If the players talked Veyrin down rather than fought him, fire the trigger the moment he drops the maul.

## The Fall

This happens whether or not the fight occurred. Trigger per the trigger above — when the party is about to be killed (combat path), or when Veyrin drops the maul (talk-down path).

Read the following slowly. Pause where indicated. Do not let the players interrupt the first three beats.

> *Veyrin's eyes go wide. He drops the maul. He puts both hands to his temples and screams — a sound with nothing in it of pride or piety or madness, only loss. The loss of the only thing that was the foundation of him.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *And then you hear it too. A sound from very far away and very far above. A great howling. A death rattle.*
>
> *Through the high east-facing windows of the sanctum, you see it pass — **a body of fire crossing the sky, moving east**. Faster than any falling star. Its light is so bright you have to look away.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *Across the temple, **every cleric of Amoth** — every acolyte, every brother, every sister, the priests in the side chapels, the one who was sweeping the cloister stones — cries out. The same cry, the same word, in the same instant. Some collapse. Some die where they stand.*
>
> *(longer pause)*
>
> *And then the floor of the world drops out from under the city.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *You feel it before you understand it. The temple shudders. Stone groans. Through the windows, the buildings of Highshelf — houses, towers, the Hall of the Companion, the streets you walked an hour ago — **fall**. Not collapse. Fall. Downward, faster and faster, into a pit opening where the city used to be. Dust and screaming rise.*
>
> *The light coming up out of the pit is the wrong color. It is brass, and fire, and very far away, but it is there, and it is climbing toward you.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *The temple does not fall.*
>
> *Some of the bluff beneath your feet shears away with the rest of the city. Some of it holds. The temple is on what holds — a tongue of basalt jutting out into emptiness. A few neighboring buildings are still here too — the chapter house, the herbarium, half of the bell tower. Everything else is gone.*
>
> *(longer pause)*
>
> *Behind you, where the headwaters of the Helgoril bubbled up out of the rocks an hour ago, the springs are dry. The cold pools where children played this morning are empty stone basins. The river is going to start flowing the wrong way. It just doesn't know it yet.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *Below you the pit opens onto something that is not Praxia. Very far down you see the orderly bronze geometry of a battlefield that goes to the horizon. You see, you think, a great metal citadel. You see things that may be devils on patrol. The Companion is gone. There is no sky.*
>
> *(pause)*
>
> *Veyrin is on his knees at the altar, hands still at his temples. The Hand of Amoth is on the floor beside him. He has stopped screaming. He has stopped doing anything. He is alive, and his eyes are open, and there is no one home.*
>
> *Behind him, the eternal flame goes out.*

End scene.

## Aftermath (if the table wants 5–10 more minutes)

The party is alive. Almost no one else they met today is.

- **No Black Helms are coming.** Lt. Aellis was on the steps of the Hall of the Companion when the city fell. So was the Hall, and so was Aellis. So were almost all the Black Helms. So was **High Observer Thavius Kreeg**, who, the players will never know, was smiling when he went down.
- **The party are now the only Black Helms left.** Their plain-clothes assignment is what saved them. Their uniforms — and the rest of the watch — are in the pit. They are now, by accident, an institution of four.
- **The temple stands.** Surviving inside it: any of the acolytes who didn't die during the cry; **Acolyte Mira** (she's at the bell tower, alive but in shock); **Brother Halis** (he was at the hospice across town and the hospice is gone, so if he's alive he's somewhere on the rim or below — DM's call); the herbalists in the chapter house; perhaps a dozen lay folk in the herbarium.
- **Veyrin** — if alive — is catatonic. He will not speak again. He has lost his god and his mind in the same moment. The acolytes don't yet know he was the killer; the party can choose to tell them or not. If they tell them, the survivors will not believe them. (Eventually they will. There are bloodstained pages under the prayer kneeler in the cell.)
- **Outside the temple**, on what is now a 30-foot tongue of basalt jutting out into emptiness: a handful of survivors are wandering in shock. Maybe forty people total — temple staff, neighbors who happened to be on the high side of the bluff.
- **Below**: the pit. Heat. Distant horns. Bronze geometry. Something very large flying very far away.
- **Behind, west**: the springs that were the headwaters of the Helgoril are dry. Within hours the river will reverse. Within a day the first water will arrive *up* the empty channel from somewhere far to the east, and pour into the pit. **The Gurges is being born.**
- **East, far away**: a glow on the horizon. The body that crossed the sky struck somewhere out there. The party will not, in this lifetime, learn what was there before it.

The party are now, accidentally, the founding survivors of what will be **[silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md)**. They do not know this. They know only that the city is gone and the temple is somehow still standing.

End the session here. Whatever they choose to do next is the start of a different campaign.

## Rewards

The 50 gp commendation Aellis promised will never be paid. Aellis is dead. The High Rider's office is dead. The Black Helms' commendation roster fell into Avernus with the rest of Highshelf. **What the party actually walks out with**:

- **Their lives.** Most of the people they spoke to today are gone.
- **Their uniforms.** They were in plain clothes for the temple but their badges and gear are at home — and home is in the pit. (DM's call: maybe a small bag of personal effects survived; maybe not.)
- **The Hand of Amoth**, if they can plausibly claim it. It is now an ordinary mithril-and-gold heavy maul (treat as a **+1 maul**, but **non-magical** until/unless a new god takes Amoth's portfolio). Worth ~600 gp as a curio — though there is no longer anyone in the city to sell it to.
- **The temple's authority** by default — they were the only law-keepers admitted by name in Veyrin's letter, and the letter is the only surviving administrative document in the city. The acolytes will defer to them.
- **Inspiration** for any player who tried to talk Veyrin down with empathy rather than force.

Material rewards do not really apply. **They are now founders of [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md).** That is the reward and the cost.

## Hooks for future sessions

- **The party are founders of [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md).** Not in the historical-record sense — in the literal sense. They are among the first ~40 mortals on the rim of what will, in 7 objective years (50 subjective; time runs strangely here from the moment the pit opens), become the elf-and-devil city of Silgorin. The first elves will arrive within weeks, the first devils within days. The Twin Councils that govern the place by the time of the notorious-rbg campaign — the **Ivory Chorus** of containment-elves and the **Brazen Compact** of contract-devils — do not yet exist. The party can choose to leave (overland; west; the route is brutal but possible), or stay and shape what Silgorin becomes.
- **The Black Helms as an institution survives in four people.** As lawkeepers in a city of forty traumatized survivors, the party will be naturally pulled toward leadership — they are the only members of any organized civic authority left on the rim. Whatever Silgorin's future governance looks like (the modern Twin Councils, per [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md), do not include an obvious peacekeeping arm), the seed of it is whatever the party decides to do over the next days. The order can fade, transform into something Silgorin needs, or — interesting — be the neutral third party between the Ivory Chorus and the Brazen Compact when those councils form.
- **The Temple of Amoth still stands.** Amoth is dead. The temple is the most sacred building in Silgorin and the only one of its kind on the rim. What the survivors decide to do with it — keep it as a memorial, repurpose it, seal it — defines the early religious character of the city. (See [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md): by the modern era there's a Temple of Fenmarel Mestarine, an Altar to Asmodeus, and the Tortured Soul tavern. None of those exist yet. The bones of one of them might be the Temple of Amoth.)
- **The Helgoril is reversing.** The party will see it begin within hours. By dawn, water will be running up the empty channel from the east. Within a day, the first water reaches the pit and begins to fall. Within a week, the falls are full. **They are watching the-gurges being born.**
- **Veyrin's journal** is in evidence. Pages near the end describe a "great red wyrm" descending and a "second sun blackening." A perceptive player will recognize the wyrm reference if they ever encounter [tiamat](/world/people/tiamat.md) or the Thargorax aspect. (Veyrin was hearing more than just Amoth dying. He was hearing what comes next.)
- **The pit goes to [avernus](/world/places/avernus.md).** Far below, the geometry of the Bronze Citadel is visible. Within days, the first **Merrenoloths** will appear on the cliff face, looking up. Within weeks, the first devils will climb out — beginning the elven/infernal coexistence that defines Silgorin. The party may be the first mortals to negotiate with them.
- **Time is starting to warp.** Per [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md), time at the rim runs roughly 7× faster than objective Praxian time. The party should notice — slightly — that the sun feels like it's moving wrong; that a conversation that took ten minutes outside felt like an hour to them; that their hair grows faster overnight. By the next session this should be unmistakable.
- **Far to the east**: the impact site (per the-fall, "just East of the Spine of the World"). They will likely never get there in person. The wave from the impact is racing west up the Helgoril channel toward them — but it has nowhere to go now except into the pit. Every village, ferry, and trading post along what was the lower river is going to be erased. If the players had contacts downriver, the news will not arrive (no one is left to bring it).
- **Down the Helgoril and out across The Gap**, on a continent the players have never heard of, in a city built on cliffs called [Aventine](/world/places/aventine.md), a different wave is racing inland. They will never know about it. But it is racing.

## Notes for the DM

- **Don't telegraph that Veyrin is the killer.** Daylight Veyrin is a kind, intelligent host with no behavioral tell. The mystery should land via **physical evidence** (the maul, the key, the rosette footprint) and **witness convergence** (Mira, Yura, Tenum) — all of which point to *something happening at night involving the High Hierophant*, not to a visibly mad man. When the realization lands at the table, it should land hard.
- **Don't let the players solve it too fast and then sit around.** If they identify Veyrin in the first hour, lean into the dread of the confrontation: he's the city's most senior holy man, beloved by his temple and his city; they have to walk past acolytes who love him; the temple goes hush as they approach.
- **Do not telegraph the cosmological scale early.** Veyrin's "Amoth is dying" speech, when it finally comes in the sanctum, should sound like the ravings of a madman. The players should have to choose whether to take him at his word, and only realize he was right about everything when the bell tolls itself.
- **The fight is not the climax.** The Fall is the climax. Build to it.
- **The fight is intentionally unwinnable.** Do not balance it down. The point is the player feeling — *we are not going to make it* — followed by the Fall. If you flinch and let the party win on dice, the moment doesn't land. Trust the trigger.
- **If a player is a cleric**, give them a scene at the moment of The Fall:
  - **Cleric of any non-Asmodean god**: feels their own god flinch. A wave of grief from elsewhere. Spells still work. Foundation just shifted.
  - **Cleric of Asmodeus**: spell save DC goes **+2** for the rest of the night. Their patron is gorging. They will know exactly what just happened and exactly who did it. Their face is theirs to play.
  - **Cleric of the Raven Queen**: a pull — they are wanted in [salmuir](/world/places/salmuir.md), or wherever their order is. Something is being put in motion that they will be needed for. (Setup for the-haddad-combine / future arcs, if the DM wants the seed.)
  - **Cleric of Amoth**: total loss of powers. Mind-wrenching. They make a **DC 15 WIS save** or join Veyrin in catatonia for the rest of the session. On a save, they can act, but they have no spells. (This will be brutal for the player. Talk to them before the session if they're playing this character.)

## Source for the Fall

See the-fall in the wiki for the canonical event. Key beats reproduced here:

- A great noise like a howling or shrill death rattle, then nothing.
- Priests/priestesses/holy warriors of Amoth lose their powers in the same moment. Some die. Others go mad.
- Followers of Asmodeus gain great power.
- Per on-the-fall-of-thargorax and the recorder's note in that document: this is **Asmodeus killing Amoth** to gain power. It works.

Veyrin is one of those holy warriors. He did not lose his powers cleanly — he lost his mind to the slow, weeks-long version of what every other Amoth-cleric in every world feels in a single instant tonight.

This scenario is also the **founding moment** of two specific places in the wiki:

- [silgorin](/world/places/silgorin.md) — the city of elves and devils on the rim of the-gurges. The wiki page describes Silgorin as "only 7 objective years old" and "formed right after the-fall." This session is hour zero of those 7 years. The temple, the few surviving buildings, and the party are what's there.
- the-gurges — the catastrophic waterfall where the Helgoril (now reversed, flowing east-to-west) plunges into the River Styx. The wiki describes it as the crater carved by The Fall. This session is the carving.

Cross-link both pages from your campaign notes — the players are now living through what those pages describe as ancient history.
