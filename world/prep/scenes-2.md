# The mission, revealed
As the players move through the ship, they'll learn more and more of Baragog and The Engine's ultimate weapon: The ship's tentacles are formidable, of course. But is its ability to unleash a barrage of psychic "spores" that makes it truly horrifying.

The spores resemble smaller mindwitnesses but have the same abilities as intellect devourers.

The ship creates the spores using the feeding pool and the larvae in the smaller pools in area 4.

Baragog spawns the larvae and uses them to generate new Illithid in Area 7, as well as the spores in Area 4.

The spores leave pods in Area 4 (two per pod). They exit the ship via the iris and await release from decks 8 and 9.

These spores are far weaker than most. They're a devastating pyschological weapon against civilian populations. They're also a powerful distraction. And, the spores can take possession of victims, building an army for the Illithid.

Baragog's plan is to unleash the spores, then use the ship's weaponry directly against House Umbra.

# General Conditions On The Engine
The ship is strangely antiseptic. Everything is made of a gray, dull material that looks like metal but feels vaguely flexible when touched. There's no smell except a faint aroma of what might be bleach. That aroma gets stronger, however, as you move towards Area 6.

Doors are irises made of the same weird material of the rest of the ship. Until alerted, Baragog lets these doors open and close automatically. Once alerted, they will attempt to strategically block doors, holding the party and allowing defenders to attack.

The doors aren't designed for security. The ship is normally crewed by 5-8 Illithid and their servants. Players can force the doors open with a DC 13 STR check.

Currently, only Baragog is controlling the ship. They have no crew except Jazelle and her minions, and they can't do much until over Aventine. Defenders include:

- Mindwitnesses
- The Psychic Golem
- Small swarms of spores

## Random Events
Roll every 15 minutes of game play, even if there's combat.

1-10:   Nothing
11-12:  Reality Shards: Razor-thin rifts to the Far Realms spin through your space. Make a DC15 DEX save. On a failure, take 1d10 slashing damage
13-17: DC 18 CHAR or WIS save (your choice). On failure, the walls appear to distort and bend around you, then rip apart, revealing your worst fears. Pick up 1pt of insanity - 2 points means short-term madness; 4 points long-term madness; 6+ means indefinite madness. See https://roll20.net/compendium/dnd5e/Madness#content for details.
18: The floor briefly turns transparent, showing you a long plunge to the ground. Make a DC 15 WIS save or fall over/grab the nearest fixed object to keep from falling. You are incapacitated for 12 seconds. Have advantage every time after the first.
19: A swarm of 6 intellect devourers emerges from a wall and attacks.
20: Everyone hand their character sheets to the left. For 30 seconds, you have your skills but you're in another player's body. Don't overthink it. Fun for the whole family.

# The Deck
Landing on the outer layer:

The outer layer is a gray, spongy "skin." It's a little sticky with some give, but unpredictable.

Turns out the schematic wasn't precisely correct. There isn't a dorsal hatch. Instead, there's a sort of weird panel that's attached to the outer skin.

The players have to figure it out. The "combination" matches the psychic runes on the original Horolith. They can attempt to remember the runes (VERY difficult) and line them up by sliding the runes up and down.

Or they can do it old school: Lock picking. If they do this, the person picking the lock and everyone within a 10' radius will take 1d12 psychic damage as the ship sounds the alarm.

However, there is a welcoming party: A Mindwitness Of Baragog. It was outside the ship on the outer deck below. When the alarm sounds, or after a minute or so, the Mindwitness drifts up and attacks. They will attempt to use Telekensis to fling the players off the surface of the ship (hopefully they're fastened on).

Environmental conditions:

- Strong winds - 30mph winds are ripping across the skin: DC 15 STR save. If fail, DC 15 Athletics checks to cling to the skin. Advantage if you're using lifelines or climbing gear. If you're in combat, make the DC15 check every round.
- Thin Air - all CON checks at disadvantage
- Thin Air - Combat exhaustion in CON/2 rounds

## When The "Hatch" Opens
The hatch is actually a massive clamshell that opens to a bowl-shaped room with a pulsing green globe partially sticking up through the floor.

_Suddenly, the outer shell vanishes. You are looking down into a small room that made of a gray, antiseptic-looking surface that might be matte metal or metallic organic material, or both. The surface is ribbed with semi-transparent tubes leading to and from a green globe that is half-hidden by the floor. The other half of the globe may be beneath the floor - that seems logical, but you can't tell. The globe pulses slowly._

When the clamshell opens, all players make DC 15 DEX saves or plunge 15'-25' to the deck below (depending on where they are on the dorsal).

Go to Area 1.

# Area 1: Collection Deck
This room is used to take on psychic "fuel" for the crew. The Nautiloid is a smaller craft that cannot carry enough sentient victims to feed both Baragog and its normal crew compliment. Instead, they process vicitms from this deck and store them in the globe that's suspended between Area 1 and 3. It can be opened during Astral travel and to receive victims which are then processed in Area 2. A field protects those on the deck from astral attacks. It has no effect when on other planes **except** you cannot leave the deck while in astral form, and astral forms cannot enter. If a creature somehow manages to be physically on the deck but astrally off the deck when the field activates, their tether is severed and they die.

In the room preparing to take an observation is another Mindwitness of Baragog. It drops what it's doing and immediately attacks.

Any players looking into the globe see brief flashes of misshapen screaming forms. These are the psychic emenations of Baragog's most recent victims. A player must make a DC18 CHAR or WIS save (your choice). On failure, they see someone recently harvested, experience their last moments, and pick up one insanity point: https://roll20.net/compendium/dnd5e/Madness#content

The clamshell is opened and closed via telepathic command and a crude panel attached to the outside of the vessel. If left open, it re-closes after 10 minutes.

Tubes run from this room through the doorway into Area 2.

# Area 2: Feeding Area
On a grotesque rounded table made of the same transparent tubing (which look more and more like vessels) rests what appears to be a stone statue of an alien creature with a large head, enormous brow, and a strange eye-like structure where its stomach might be. In the place of "hands" are two pulsing green globes.

Around the statue are eight smaller green globes.

This statue is a channeler for Illithid rituals. If the players each touch a globe, they immediately swap bodies. It is also a psychic golem. If the players attempt to interact with the table or statue (including activating the ritual stones) the golem awakens and attacks.

Stairs down lead to Area 3.

# Area 3: Extraction and Storage
Lower half of globe. The doors in this area are all broken down, and the entire deck is wide open. There's no one here. But there is a strengthening thrumming sound, and the players are certain they're being followed.

The globe itself hangs, radiator spikes trailing down into the room from the ceiling. If the players touch the spikes, they make the same rolls with the same effects as staring into the globe in Area 1.

They hear faint whispers, as if Jazelle were speaking to them. They can't quite make out the words. If they concentrate, on a DC 20 INT check they hear, "This isn't what we thought we wanted we thought we needed we wanted we thought the monster "

The entire ship is starting to bank slowly right as it prepares to release its deadly cargo.

# Area 4: Breeding Area
This is where Baragog spawns the spores. Each of the small cubbies at one end are open. Dozens of umbilicals hang, recently disconnected from their spores.

Iris leads out onto the deck (area 8).

The iris is open. The ship is banking gently to the right and angled down, so they can see Aventine in the distance. They can also see countless of the spores swarming from the ship towards the city.

_If the players haven't fought the spores yet, you may want to have them tangle with one or two so they can see what the spores are capable of._

They're too late. The attack has begun. The players can only watch in horror.

Then, just as (hopefully) they've despaired, a small grouping of arrows kills two spores. A shadow passes in front of the iris, and a small flight of wyverns swoops past in pursuit. Four of the ship's tentacles lash out, killing one wyvern, but then a barrage ballista bolts sprout from the base of two of the tentacles. The players hear Grimshaw's swearing as the Wave Serpent passes rapidly overhead, unable to keep up with Lor Manàdh.

Narrow stairs on either side lead down to Area 4.

# Area 6: The Nerve Center
A long, narrow room with eight small spawning pools.

Jazelle and Arvid are here. One look tells the players that Jazelle and Arvid aren't quite right. Their eyes are white. Their flesh has taken on a grayish tinge, and there are tiny networks of burst veins around their eyes and mouths. They haven't been implanted yet, but they've been mind blasted for days. Jazelle was already nearly insane because of Baragog's influence.

When the players enter, they see Baragog floating in its pool as Jazelle and Arvid prepare spells.

Two Baragog's left is the ship's socket. The elder brain has one fleshy tentacle wrapped around the socket, which holds the Horolith.

The moment the players enter, Baragog pummels them. They are paralyzed as the elder brain assaults them with images, trying to gain control. Baragog speaks to them, showing them a peaceful Aventine free of House Umbra safe from the horrors of another Shard War.

Jazelle and Arvid do not attack while the elder brain tries to take control.

The very next round, the players can make DC 18 WIS saves. On a save, they are immediately freed, and Jazelle and Arvid attack. The other players remain paralyzed until they make their saves, or until Baragog takes 40 damage.

**Be very careful with this encounter. Baragog is massive, and Jazelle and Arvid alone could kill the party. Arvid is constrained because he can't cast his most potent spells in the ship. Jazelle isn't a combatant. But both will stand down until Baragog orders them into the fight. Play it by ear.**

Ways to win:

1. Kill Baragog. If the elder brain dies, the ship dies with it and begins to fall from the sky. The players have some time to escape, as the Automaton will keep the ship from dropping suddenly. But, the ship's defenses continue operating, and the players may be attacked by spores as they try to leave.
2. Insert the false key. The players can spend two rounds attempting to remove Baragog's tentacle from the Horolith by inflicting 30 points of damage to it. If they do so, they can then remove the Horolith with a DC 20 Thieves Tools, Tinkering, or STR check. Any player attempting to remove the Horolith is barraged with images of Illithid attacks on cities, taking 5 psychic damage each round. They must make a DC 15 WILL save to keep a hold of the Horolith. When the false key is inserted, the entire Automaton begins to separate from The Engine. The players hear a rending crash and the ship plunges like a stone. They have a few rounds to get out the iris or otherwise out of the ship.
3. Otherwise do so much damage to The Engine that it can no longer fly. They might destroy an entire chamber as well as the hull, remove more than 1/2 the ship's tentacles, or separate the Automaton from The Engine.


# Area 7: The Laboratory
_This room reeks of ammonia and chlorine and is lit by a sickly green glow. You're standing at the top of a series of four platforms. On the right and left side each platform are two pools of liquid. These pools are the source of both the light and the smells in the room._

_In each pool is a humanoid form. The bodies in the first three rows are nearly dissolved, gray flesh hanging flaccidly from barely-recognizable skeletons. Swarms of some type of larvae are attached around the ears of each figure._

_In the last two pools are two of Jazelle's followers. They're still intact and may even be alive. They're twitching feebly in the pools. If you look carefully, the tail of a creature protudes from each person's left ear. The Illithid are attempting to multiply using their bodies._

If Baragog is dead, then Jazelle's followers have half-dragged themselves from their tanks and will try to claw feebly at the characters are they pass.

Chances are the entire ship is heeling, spilling brine from the tanks, so the floor is slippery. All players make a DC 15 DEX save to avoid falling. If they fall, they take 1 point of bludgeoning damage (because the ship is shaking and they bounce of off various objects as they slide across the floor).

# Area 8: Airlock
If Baragog is dead, this iris is half-open, half-closed. Players can squeeze through if they take their time. If they rush, they need to make a STR 15 check (athletics OK) or get stuck for 1 round.

# Area 9: The Outer Deck
The wind is screaming by as the doomed ship starts spiraling downward. Pieces of the hull are flying past, and the massive tentacles flail around them.

Every round, the ship accelerates downward. The wind tears at the players, making it harder and harder to hang on. If Jazelle and Arvid are still alive, they pursue the characters. Continue the combat.

Regardless, the ship has slowed enough that the Kestrel (Surprise) can pull overhead and rescue the survivors. Wyvern riders can also grab them.
