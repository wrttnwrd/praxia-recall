The "city" is a giant cave filled with crypts, graves, and the tools and equipment necessary to harvest flesh and souls for the Flayed God.

Most of those interred here "volunteered" to give up their flesh to preserve, then revive, Amenopophis. In some cases, entire families traveled to the city because of a mother, father, or child who persuaded them to take the journey. Once there, they were harvested using the tools and processes develped by Ammsu.

All were told that, when Amenopophis rises, they will be restored to life to serve by his side.

## Buildings & Appearance
The City has a combination of buildings ranging from small, single-tombstone graves to massive crypts. The eastern side is bare graves and small tombs for those who couldn't afford more.

The cave walls are partially-collapsed rubble. In them are countless cubbies, recesses, and loose rocks. 

Most of these contain flayed, mummified corpses. Some contain skulls and bones of loved ones. There is very little writing - few of the people here were literate.

The entire city has been damaged. 

## Environment
Lit by everburning torches and braziers, light flickers and dances over everything. All perception checks are at -2 because of the inconsistent lighting. Darkvision doesn't help.

It is cool and very, very dry, and smells vaguely dusty until the players make their way to the Loom, which "feels" humid (but has no smell at this level). 

The City is the first sign of the scale of what has happened here. Thousands upon thousands of living beings, brought here as material for The Loom. The "streets" are packed with "people" going about their business, doing random activities, wandering and weeping as they attempt to free themselves, or in some cases, waiting excitedly for Amenopophis to rise.

At some point, people did live here. There are signs of it everywhere: Ancient tables with cracked dishes, worn cobbles in the streets, even remains of small stone houses. 

Random things the party might see/hear. Feel free to have random fear effects depending on what they see:

1. A group of people walking by, chatting animatedly
2. A single woman, sighing sadly while stroking a beautiful gem
3. Random whispers: "Quiet! They'll hear (giggle)", "Something is wrong", "We haven't seen them in days," "There's no news," "Where do YOU think they went," "He has left us," "We are all cursed," "I'm telling you, there's nothing to worry about," "Where is my SON?!", "These musty corridors hold our cursed dead", "The Loom Eternal weaves", "His servants lie sleepless, deathless. LOOK" (after this quote, have creepy crawlies come out)
4. A ghostly head emerges from a tomb or grave as if pulling to escape something. It screams/howls and then appears to be pulled back in
5. A small child/halfling beckons to the party, then disappears around a building or large stone. If the party follows, there's nothing there. When they turn, it's right above them, screaming. Once the party responds, it runs away, laughing
6. Guards: https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/175405-nightveil-specter

As the players get closer to the Loom, they will see:

1. An increasing number of apparitions struggling, as if being dragged towards something against their will
2. Looms, still in operation, taking "thread" from the spinning wheels and weaving it into the stronger sinews. These smaller looms are magically taking the thread. There are no apparent pulleys or gears - just perfectly straight lines of a faintly-glowing, pale greenish string (flesh that's been processed) going in, and a strip of strong, woven flesh going out
3. A Swarm Of Hands: First a vague scrabbling sound coming from cracks in the floor, then what appear to be the hands of people struggling to get out. But as they emerge, players realize these are **only** hands. There are dozens of them, of all different shapes and sizes. They inflict 1d4 damage (+9 to hit) as they crawl over the players, scratching and scraping them with filthy nails and claws.


## Flora and Fauna
There's nothing down here.

## Random
- The city is occupied by the ghosts and wights of those who went to serve Amenopophis. Most were willing, some were not. Most of the willing slumber in their graves. A few are servants and guards for Amenopophis. They will act to stop intruders. 
- The unwilling move about the city, trapped there by the process that fed Amenopophis 



## The Tombs
There are several higher-end Tombs.

Many contain goodies.

All are trapped.

Some have residents that don't want intruders.

Some have residents that enjoy a chance to converse.

A few examples to use:

### 2. Unidentified Remains
A perfectly preserved, dried out mummy wearing a diamond tiara worth 1000gp. There is no magic.

### 3. Swarm Of Hands Nest
A small nest full of hands. If Amenopophis is dead, the hands have torn each other apart. Mixed into the remains are a few rings worth a total of 500gp.

#### 4. Samuel & Gerald
Wealthy couple interred here. Trapped with a basic crossbow. A few pearls worth 50gp.

Also in this tomb are three skeletons, partially mummified with age. They're still wearing armor. 

These are three adventurers who found the city 200 years ago. The adventurers (unnamed) caught The Curse Of The Flayed God and slowly died. There are still dried up bits of skin lying around their bodies.

There are also long-decayed wineskins.

They also have:
1. Three scrolls of Purify Food And Drink - that was how they continued to eat while here
2. Several blunted, corroded weapons
3. The [Wildseed](https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/5540557-the-wildseed) - actually not in their possession, but immediately apparent to the party's barbarian. It was placed here by Nik'thim in their effort to help the party stop Amenopophis

#### 5. Ezekiel The Mad
A caster who came willingly, believing this to be a ticket to eternity. Now haunts the chamber, somewhat insane but harmless.

No treasure.

#### 6. The Secret Tomb Of Ellechar
The wealthiest of all who came to Amenopophis's aid.

Ellechar - a Yuan-Ti of great influence in the Church of Setth - funded much of the construction of The City.

He was also the cartographer of Amenopophis. He researched and helped plan the Flayed God's first conquests after he re-emerges. He quickly learned the urgency of those first conquests, realizing that the Flayed God was racing against some unknown, coming calamity (The Fall) that would hasten the rise of the forces of Hell.

In exchange, he was granted "eternal life" as a powerful flayed mummy https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/276630-gorka-tharn (minus legendary actions) and given a place on the Council. But he became frustrated, disenchanted with what he saw as needless delays to bring about Amenopophis's rise and conquests.

The rest of the council considered him a blasphemer and eventually locked him in his tomb. They concealed the door and trapped the room, hoping to keep Ellechar from leaving.

When Ellechar hears the players, he shouts for help. His voice is raspy and weak, but they can barely hear him. Any PC with a passive PER > 19 can faintly hear something.

After years of disuse, the door is clear enough. But it's hard to figure out how to get it open. DC 25 lock picking will do it. Or persuade me.

Once they open the door, they'll see Ellechar at the opposite end of the room, standing at a platform. He is speaking ancient Salmuirian so accented that no one can understand him, saying leetzpah malkodet (the floor is a trap). He's gesturing frantically at the floor.

_Once he realizes he's speaking to 'commoners' he will adopt Common (still accented) and be more understandable to the party._

The room is a trap:

The entire room has a pivoting floor above a lake of quicksilver. 

DC 25 to see the trap. Once triggered, players have 1 round to make a DC 25 DEX, STR, or WIS save to find a way to stop the floor or find a safe spot. Failure means they slide down the floor as it tilts, landing in the quicksilver pool below.

Fall in and start making CON saves, DC15 every round. Each failed save brings additional symptoms:

1. -2 STR
2. -2 CON
3. 10hp lost from internal bleeding
4. 20hp lost from internal bleeding
5. Temporary madness: Fear, paranoia, etc.
6. Tremors
7. -2 DEX
8. A metallic taste in your mouth

Roll a d8 to see which symptoms the PCs get. If CON goes to 0, the character dies.

If the players fall through the floor, the floor immediately pivots back up, sealing them in. Other PCs can pivot the floor, or the trapped PC can make a DC 22 STR check to push the floor up again. Then they need to figure out how to pin the floor in place or otherwise scramble up.

If the players cross the room to Ellechar, he is not hostile. Once they're close, he attempts to speak to them in Common. He blames the Inner Circle for his imprisonment. He will truthfully answer any questions the party asks him.

Also: Ellechar doesn't know that he's dead. He thinks he's only recently been entombed. 

If the players attempt to leave him there, he freaks out. He's no match for the party and just tries to get past them. He babbles the entire time about "not being trapped ever again."

He may trigger the trap and vanish beneath the floor. The players may destroy him. They may set him free. Or they may lock him in.

If they set him free, he capers away, cackling joyfully. Then he looks at the state of The City and realizes what's happened. His hands come up to his face. He shrieks with despair and runs. They don't see him again.

##### Treasure
This room is literally a treasure trove. Everything Ellechar wanted to take to the next life, he put here. There are:

- 2500 gp worth of diamonds
- 1000 gp of gold in small sacks
- Beautiful silks - 4500 gp
- Canopic jars containing most of Ellechar's now-shriveled organs
- Jars of now-500-year-old wine - undrinkable, mostly evaporated
- A small, polished obsidian menagerie with all sorts of creatures. Not magical. Almost priceless, but hard to sell
- A pile of maps, one of which shows Porth Mor. Let the players figure out what that may mean


### 8. Burial Chamber Of Galdrowynn Lightjaw
Dwarf, buried here long ago. 

Galdrowynn came here full of hope for the promise of Amenopophis. She felt betrayed in the end, though, and ingested a powerful neurotoxin before being flayed. The toxin remained in her system and now covers her remains.

If the players touch or inhale the powder, make an immediate DC18 CON save. Take 10d6 poison damage or half as much on a successful save. On a failed save, the player is poisoned and suffers another 4d6 damage every 1 minute until protection from poison, an antidote, Lesser Restoration, or Lay On Hands (or something more powerful).

### 7.  The Loom, Top
At the far end of the City is the top of the Loom.

The loom looks like it's been cobbled together. That's due to repairs made after The Fall.

It's surrounded by equipment and dissection tables similar to the Flayed Workshop, but the ruins of at least 40-50 of them. Clearly dozens of mummifications could happen here at once. Now, only 17 tables remain.

The tables were clearly built for medium humanoids, with restraints and molds to match. Now, they hold a nightmarish, random collection of once-living things:

- A yuan-ti, still partly covered in skin
- Completely skinned animals of varying types
- An unidentifiable, small humanoid, also skinned
- A very recently dead gray elf
- If the players look around, describe other creatures

At the end of each table is a spinning wheel. Flesh is magically pulled into the spinning wheel, turned to thread, then spun out across the city into the looms.

The top of the Loom resembles a large funnel made from polished stone. Above it is a rapidly-spinning, impenetrably dark globe. The strips fed into it from around the city emerge from the bottom as a single, 1-2 foot wide piece of fabric. The top of the Loom pulses with a sickly green light.

Other items at the top of the Loom:

- Excerbrators, used to remove brains
- Canopic jars, empty or filled with badly decayed, dried-up preservatives
- Chains, encrusted in long-dried blood

The Inner Circle took their most precious possessions into the chamber below.

When the players first arrive, there are no signs of life except a [brain in a jar](https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/194139-brain-in-a-jar-noncore). Once destined for the Inner Circle, Yitzach performed hundreds of mummifications, feeding bits into The Loom. Yitzach was imprisoned here by Ammsu. The brain is quite mad, but will engage in conversation if possible. If the players attempt to leave, Yitzach becomes enraged and attacks. Players can walk away, or destroy the jar.

If the players wait long enough, though, a series of flesh automatons - reduced-threat wights without volition - proceed into the room to maintain the tables and spinning wheels and check on the status of the various specimens.

The wights don't attack or defend themselves.

##### Damaging or destroying The Loom
If the players don't shut down the Loom but kill Amenopophis, he will eventually regenerate.

The rapidly-spinning globe is a Globe Of Mortification. It converts flesh to the stuff needed for Amenopophis's new body. Anyone touching the globe must make an immediate DC 20 CHAR save. On failure, the character takes 80 points of necrotic damage. If reduced to 0hp, they die instantly but can be restored with revivify, etc.

Touching the globe or casting a > level 3 spell within 50' of the globe, or coming within 25' of the globe, triggers it. Combat opens. 

The globe has 300hp. It is immune to all non-magical damage. It's resistant to magic weapons and spells and reflects damage back to the user. Have them roll a d20:

1-10: No damage
11-15: Full damage
16-20: Double damage

The Globe takes full damage from Radiant damage.

The Globe has two attacks per round:

1. Necromantic Lightning: +8 to hit, 2d12 necrotic damage
2. Madness: DC 15 CHAR or the player is *stunned* for one round. Each round they're stunned, the sense that their skin just somehow doesn't belong grows stronger. On the third failure, they begin trying to peel off their flesh, inflicting 1d6 points of damage on themselves. They can attempt a DC 15 INT at the end of their next round to recover. If they recover, reset the clock. If they don't, they continue inflicting damage.

Players have two ways to destroy the Globe: 

1. They can destroy it directly, fighting it per the stats above; or
2. They can destroy the feeders, in reverse order. The destruction of each rune follows the powers of each of the Inner Circle. If they destroy the runes in the correct order, **none of these things happen.** If they destroy them in the wrong order, the effects bdlow happen. regardless, if they successfully destroy the runes, then the Globe's HP fall to 150, and it cannot reflect damage
	1. Alpeh: Necromantic Fog - https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/cloudkill
	2. Bet: Necromantic Fire - https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/incendiary-cloud but 5d8, 20ft area
	3. Gimmel: Necromantic Bile - https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/acid-splash at level 17
	4. Dalet: Necromantic Silt - https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/erupting-earth, 4d12 damage


Either way, whe the globe is destroyed, it triggers a necrotic blast: 50' radius, 5D12 Necrotic, DEX save for half damage.

If the players are all incapacitated, they awaken on tables in the Inner Circle's chambers. They're thoroughly bound to the tables. Peering down at them are the Necromancers Of The Flayed God.

If the players win and destroy the Globe or the tables in the right order, they see the opening to the Inner Circle and can descend. The Necromancers are alerted and are beginning to awaken.

Fade to Black.

The mouth of the Loom glows but it just a hole down to the Inner Circle's chambers.
