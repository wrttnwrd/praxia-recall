## Recap
1. Mossy and Drak began the night tussling with a Troll Titan. It got a few good shots in, but a few well-placed thorns, firebolts, and arrows (plus an inaugural Eldritch Blast), and a ballista shot, he went down like, well, a giant troll that bit off way too much. Drak ended up with some of his insides on the outside but no one ended up unconscious. With some careful knot-tying and a light touch, [the Villain's Discontent](/world/vehicles/the-villains-discontent.md) lifted the Seal off the ground. But a gaggle of pissed-off Duergar ran out a new ballista that looked like it could've punched a giant hole the airship. The ship's wizard, Opius (YES HE HAS A NAME NOW) decided that was the perfect time to use the ship's spell cannon. He flipped a Fireball into the barrel which proceeded to nuke the entire mine, reducing all humanoids to singed articles of footwear and a few bits of armor. Unfortunately, Opius had NOT trained on the cannon. He rolled a 1 on a d4, directing 20% of the damage back on himself and the ship. The ship is charred and has some burnt rigging but is otherwise OK. Opius and Cregyn are charred and have some burnt rigging as well. And Opius is on a time out. The party then took a careful look at the Seal. Mossy realized this is actually some kind of container with a lock, and that there's something inside. She was tempted to open it. The entire party said um, no, let's not. You're still talking about next steps. At the same time, Sango made an appearance! He showed up at the mansion scrounging for food. After a Looney-Tunes-worthy tour of Mossy's traps, he wandered the city, mumbling about getting home, a compass, and some person/place/thing no one could figure out. Artemis Hasp recommended a good therapist, and Thistlebeard is worried that one of his employers ate too many mushrooms. And that's where we left off.

## The Plot
Wrapping up stuff from last session.

- The Troll Titan is a problem, but they can handle it
- The bigger issue will be the steady flow of Duergar and Derro

The Seal is actually inside the globe they're lifting. An Arcana or Artificer check will reveal that the globe is some kind of enclosure. 

They can smash it open - it has an AC 15 and 100hp. Doing so risks cracking the Seal. The Seal has a 10 CON save and, as long as it makes it, it's OK.

**If the Seal cracks** - This seal is Divinity. Cracking it splits clerics from specific gods that hold the line between the Material and other planes: Helm, The Raven Queen,  and immediately summons a https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/16980-planetar Planatar who attacks without hesitation. The Planatar is maddened and acts as a Fallen Planatar. All clerics and paladins of these gods take 2d10 Psychic damage. If they make a Wisdom save, that's all. If they fail, they are unable to call upon the divine powers - spells, lay on hands, etc., but not Turn Undead - of their god until they make the save. They can make the save every 24 hours. They know the magic is there - their connection to their god isn't broken. There's just something blocking them.

An artificer with lock picking can give it a shot: Three rolls DC 25. Only artificers, not rogues, normal 2X proficiency assuming proficiency w/ thieves tools or artificer tools.

An artificer who creates a potent acid could potentially dissolve the lock, as well.

If they successfully unlock it, the Seal is inside. It is a one-foot-wide disk made of some kind of stone with a symbol on it:
