## Recap
1. Last game: After the party battled an Abishai hunting party, Cregyn broke the game with Divine Intervention. White beams of light shot from the "sky" of Avernus, revealing the location of Tiamat's Monument and Arkhan's Tower. Remember that the Tower presents the easiest way past the swarms of Abishai and dragons guarding the Monument and into Tiamat's chamber. Red beams marked two dangers: A watchtower and an unidentified site. And, most important, Cregyn's god pointed out Bazett, who you later discovered is a somewhat-batty Ultraloth. You took shelter for the night, nearly detected by Urush Ghul while in the vault beetle. The next day, you headed for Bazett. He was suspended in a cage over a huge lake of demon ichor. The wizard Mordenkainen imprisoned Bazett there 250 years ago. Bazett has hung there, breathing in the fumes, ever since. You retrieved the Ultraloth and his anti-teleportation manacles. (The lake is apparently a demon lord named Shumroth who's been subjected to a steady drip from the River Styx - there's a story there, you're pretty sure) Bazett gave you a few important bits of information:
    
    1. The only way to strengthen the Chains Of Gleynir is some form of reality-altering magic. The chains are ancient artifacts of great power.
    
    2. There are a few ways to get to Arkhan's Tower. You chose the Darking Way, a neglected tunnel that passes under the River Styx and emerges next to Arkhan's Tower. It's full of creatures nasty enough to keep the fiends out, but it's still better than dodging highly-trained hunting parties and dragons for two weeks.
    
    3. He'll take you to the Tower, but not in.
    
    Right then, Urush Gûl caught you. The ancient red dragon, scarred by his confrontation with Tiamat and enraged at the party, swooped in and attacked. His fire breath had no effect on you (but did badly wound Bazett). You fled, taking shelter in the ruins of a castle. You found a dead-end passage and hid there. Urush is flying back and forth, pounding on the rubble, trying to find a way in. You figured out:
    
    4. There are two quarter-mile-high towers nearby. They're too big to topple, but you might be able to rig nearby rubble piles to fall on the dragon if he's on the ground. Or, you can try to drop the bridge on him if he flies under it. Tricky.
    
    5. The Hand That Strikes would kill Gûl but exacts a high price.
    
    6. This is the biggest foe you've faced, but your invulnerability to fire (ironically a boon Gûl granted) nullifies one of his most potent weapons
    
    Monday will start with the battle.
