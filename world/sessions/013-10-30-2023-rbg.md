This is a one-shot for Halloween. Integrate the happenings as desired.

## The Plot
A cult of Zuggtmoy seeks thralls it can use to capture more of the Underdark. To do that, it sends Fungal Ambassadors into camps, villages, and wherever humanoids have gathered. By releasing clouds of mind-altering spores, the Ambassador captures the minds of unconscious victims and casts them into a kind of labyrinth designed to take their sanity. The stress of the experience weakens victims.

**Zuggtmoy's Embrace.** If a victim "dies" in the labyrinth, their bodies are taken by the Ambassador's spores and turned into fungal thralls. 

**Spore Servants** The players will encounter all kinds of partly-converted or completely converted spore servants. These grotesque things have almost no intelligence, but they do have some advantages. See https://dnd-5e.fandom.com/wiki/Spore_Servant. When hit, they release clouds of spores that require a DC 10 CON save. On a failure, the player is poisoned. The effect disappears in one round.

The players are sleeping in their cave. A weird, balloon-looking creature with a beak on top and tentacles seemingly blunders into their camp:

### The Fungal Ambassador
_You hear a shuffling, snuffling sound outside the cave. Suddenly, an enormous, leathery, balloon-shaped monstrosity blunders into the cave. It looks like a huge, bulbous torso without a head. It wears no clothing, and its flesh is pockmarked with tufts of unhealthy, greasy looking hair. Two arms poke out of the "shoulders," but the arms are out of proportion. One arm has an enormous, inflated-looking hand. The other is bony, with flaps of flesh hanging off it. The legs are short, jutting out of giant folds of skin that wrap around where the thing's hips should be. The creature reeks of decayed and mortifying flesh._ 

If the players look closely, they also see:

_Then you notice it's not just a torso. Peering out of mountains of puffy flesh are two beady eyes and the very tip of a nose. Between the thing's "shoulders" is the very top of a misshapen head. Its as if a humanoid torso swelled up, enveloping much of the head and lower extremities._

If the players attack, a single HP causes it to burst, releasing a thick cloud of spores. If not, the ambassador charges into the cave and explodes.

Either way, sleeping players have no save. They drift deeper into sleep. Waking players can make a DC20 CON save. On a success, they're trapped in the cult's realm for 2 hours. On a fail, they're trapped until they die or find their way out.

Everyone starts with 5 sanity points. Different events cost sanity points. If sanity points reach zero, the player jerks awake and falls under the spore infection. They don't know it yet. Use this as desired. Once discovered, the infection can be cured using lesser restoration, cure disease, or greater restoration.

Losing sanity points:

1. Spell/trap effects
2. Falling to zero hp
3. Each hour spent in the labyrinth on a failed DC 18 WIS save

### The Madness Of Zuggtmoy
The labyrinth's otherworldly environment saps the sanity of all within it. Each hour, characters must make a DC18 WIS save. If they fail, they lose a sanity point.

Anyone in the labyrinth who loses a sanity point (including the players) experiences symptoms in this order:

1. Develop a faint orange tinge to the skin on their face and hands
2. Small bumps break out on the head and face
3. Head begins to enlarge and round
4. Eyes start to recede into face
5. Mouth turns into a jack o lantern leer. 


## Conditions
The ground releases a small puff of brown dust with each step. Anyone attempting to sneak gets advantage against detection by sound, but creatures attempting to find them using sight have advantage on perception.

Each room has a different kind of fungus. Lighting is generated accordingly.

## Locations
Read when players first arrive.

You awaken lying on the ground. It's dry and spongy. The air is saturated with a sickly, sweet smell like old vegetable matter. The entire area around you is lit by a faint, green glow.

As you look around, you see you're in some kind of chamber. The walls are a dry-looking, brown material that appears to be covered in small bumps that glow dimly.

You hear a female voice in your heads: "Welcome, and congratulations. You are chosen. Explore my labyrinth and see the shape of things to come."

### 1.  Labyrinth Entrance
A large cave that's divided up by large fungal growths. 

In it are two other humanoids: A duergar and a sverfneblin. They're clearly about to come to blows.

The duergar is a woman named Maermura. The sverfneblin is a male named Thelwald. Each blames the other for "trapping" them here, even though that's absurd. They're both speaking Undercommon.

**Lighting**: Pale green

### 2. Pustule Chamber
This chamber is lined with pustules on the walls and floor. See below. 

The pustules are a kaleidoscope of colors, from red to green to purple. It's lit in a variety of colors.

The Avatar created this chamber to begin the process of breaking her victims.

Also in this room is another victim - a Drow - in stage 1 of their transformation.

### 3. The Zoo
The Avatar's first few attempts at trapping creatures were, uh, unsuccessful. She brought in all manner of non-sentient creatures, including a cave bear. 

### 3a. The Bear Trap Bear Trap
This chamber is lit yellow by smooth, featureless walls that give slightly to the touch.

Zuggtmoy turned the bear into a spore servant and has kept it as a pet. 

But one of the first sentient humanoids lured into the maze was a famous sverfneblin hunter named Toglal Jointvein. Toglal tracked the bear and laid a complex trap, only to go insane shortly thereafter. The trap includes a conventional bear trap and a wall "pit." 

The bear stepped around the bear trap (cleverly) only to be trapped in the wall (as planned). Now, a bear trap lies partly concealed in the floor. There is no way around the trap. If the players tamper with it, it's deactivated, but the wall slides open and the bear attacks.

It is only barely recognizable as a bear. Covered with fungus, only its size and shape give it away.

Use a juiced-up polar bear for stats: https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/16983-polar-bear but remember the spore attack.

### 3b. Worm Warrens
Light: Dark, only what the players bring with them.

This section isn't actually a chamber. It's a set of 10-foot-high tunnels carved through the "meat" of this place. 

When the players move 10 feet into the area, they see a half-dissolved body in stage 2 of the transformation.

A purple worm was trapped here when Zuggtmoy created the place. 

Turn this down as needed: https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/16987-purple-worm

Ideally, creatures lured into the labyrinth end up in this set of chambers, where potent spores cause them to lose their way and slowly sap their remaining sanity.

As the players move through section:

2. Random infected move past. Some run headlong, screaming. Others stumble and shamble. Still others seem perfectly normal, until you look more closely and realize they're in various phases - mostly 3 and 4 - of their transformation.


### 6. Holding Chamber
_You see that the passage widens a bit ahead and glows with a rich, red light. You can hear a mix of voices - low sighs, babbling, even some laughter._

The walls in this section are a pale, dull pink with dots of shiny purple and red. The dots glow brightly, creating the light everyone sees.

The chamber is packed with thralls who have lost all vestiges of their sanity. They're fully transformed.

The thralls make no move to attack. They do not resist if the players attack them. But, if killed, they explode, inflicting 4hp force damage and releasing a cloud of spores similar to wall pustules: DC15 WIS save or lose a sanity point.

### 6a. The Room Of Failures
Some labyrinth visitors don't go insane or escape. They die. When that happens, their bodies magically teleport to this room.

The ground is lined with large, rich-looking fungus and mushrooms. With a 15PER the players see that the fungi are growing on a layer of dead bodies. If any players died previously, their body is here.

### 8. The Avatar's Lair
At the center of the labyrinth, designed to break anyone who's made it this far. In it is the Avatar of Zuggtmoy. 



## Random Encounters
Every 15 minutes, choose or roll 1d20. On a 10-20, choose or roll below:

1: All players make a DC 15 Survival check. On a fail, they step into a ground pustule and sink up to their shins. The ground appears to grasp at them, making weird, dry sucking sounds (think a dental suction tube). Make a DC 13 STR/Athletics check to escape. If anyone helps, get +4 per person helping

2: The party walks past someone who lost their sanity and are now thralls in the waking world. In this place, their half-digested body is trapped in the wall, floor, or ceiling, and lets out a piercing shriek with no effect

3: Make a DC20 Perception check. Otherwise a wall pustule bursts, releasing spores. Roll a DC15 WIS save or lose one sanity point

4: 1d6 Duergar spore servants attack

5: 1d6 Sverfneblin spore servants attack

6: Bear trap: A large bear trap is concealed in the middle of the hall with three severed legs. A relatively easy PER roll (15+) detects it. There's no easy way around it. When you disable it, a large bear spore servant falls from the ceiling and attacks

## Tidbits
If the players succeed, any surviving Duergar and Sverfneblin will, upon waking, remember that the PCs helped rescue them. That could be important later, as the party tries to enter Ironstead and get the third Seal.

In addition, there's a chance the players will encounter a thrall in their travels. Passive PER 18 and they'll have a chance of recognizing them.

## Avatar Of Zuggtmoy
As Zuggtmoy, but no legendary actions or resistances, no spells, no infestation spores. Mind control spores require DC 12 save.

_This room is hot and humid. Milling about are about a dozen barely-identifiable humanoid figures so far gone that you can't figure out their race or species. Their heads have been completely replaced with the bizzare, orange fungus you've seen on the other lost souls in the labyrinth. But where their heads once were, these victims have almost perfectly round lumps of bright orange growth. One of them turns towards you and you're horrified to see that whatever visage they once had has been replaced by a leering, toothless grin and a pair of deep-set, lightless eyes. They all turn in unison and begin gibbering, "For her. All for her. All for her. All for her..."_

_Then you see her. Stepping from the shadows is a seven-foot-tall female form. At first you think she may be elvish or even fey, with pale, almost gray skin and thin features. She is graceful and has long, flowing hair. In the context of the last few hours, she seems refreshingly normal._

_Alas. As she's completely revealed, you realize with growing horror that she is, in fact, made entirely of fungus. Her skin is grey, rubbery, glistening. What you thought was hair is gently waving filaments of some kind, very similar to the fine layers of red fungus that form her dress. Her blouse is a webbing of fungal flesh, perforated with tiny holes that puff out a continuing cloud of fine, powdered spores.

_"Welcome" she breathes, "you've arrived just in time."_

Zuggtmoy explains she's there recruiting an army of "volunteers." She promises "peace, and a sense of belonging" to anyone who joins her. 

Assuming the players don't cooperate, she attacks immediately, using her protective thralls and spores.

If the party dies, they wake up, but they're infected.
